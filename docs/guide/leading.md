# Leading an ensemble

Leaders – teachers, conductors, choir directors – look after the ensembles they lead. Admins and librarians create
ensembles and decide who leads them; everything else on this page leaders do themselves. Leaders can also browse the
whole library and open every part.

*Ensembles* lists your ensembles; *My music* shows the ones you lead under *You lead*.

## Players

On the ensemble's page under **Members**:

- *Add a person …* adds someone who already has an account. Admins can also *Invite someone new*.
- For each member, *+ instrument* sets what they play, with a part number or *all*: "Clarinet 2" sees only the
  second clarinet part, "Clarinet (all parts)" every clarinet part. Someone can play several instruments.
- *Remove* takes them out of the ensemble (the account stays).

Members of an ensemble see only themselves in its member list – pupils are often minors.

**Single parts:** sometimes one person needs one more part – the full score for the accompanist, the second part for
someone helping out. Under *Single parts* choose the person and the part and *Grant*; *Take back* ends it. It also
ends when the person leaves the ensemble or the work leaves it.

## Works

*Add a work from the library …* gives a work to the ensemble; from then on every member sees their parts of it.
Taking a work away also takes it out of the ensemble's programmes.

## Appearance

Under *Appearance* (on the ensemble's page) a larger ensemble gets its **logo**; a small one a **symbol** – an emoji
on one of eight colours – or its initials. The logo wins while there is one.

## Programmes

A programme is a concert or a set list: name, date, start time, venue and notes, and the pieces in playing order.
*New programme, e.g. Spring concert* creates one.

- Add works of the ensemble (adding a work from elsewhere gives it to the ensemble) and **breaks** such as an interval
  or a speech, with a duration if you like.
- Reorder by dragging or with the arrows; each item can have a note ("with repeat", "soloist: …").
- The card shows when each item starts, the total and the expected end. Works without a duration are named – enter
  it right there; it is stored with the work.
- **Running order** is a printable page with numbers, times, composer, arranger and durations.
- **Set list** is the list for the performing rights society (in Germany the GEMA *Musikfolge*): event, and per work
  title, composer, lyricist, arranger, publisher, work number and duration – printable and as a CSV file. Works with
  missing details are listed with links. It follows GEMA's paper forms; it is not GEMA's own form.

Members see each programme on *My music* with their parts in playing order, and the viewer turns from one piece to
the next.

## Guest links

A substitute who has no account gets a **guest link**: on the ensemble's page under *Guest links*, *New guest link …*.

- *For whom* – a name, e.g. "Max, trumpet substitute".
- The parts – one by one, or *All parts of* an instrument and part number in a programme.
- *Works until* – suggested is the day after the next concert, otherwise two weeks; at most a year.

The link is **shown only once**: copy it and send it on. Lost it? Stop it and make a new one. The guest can open the
parts in the viewer and download them – no markings, nothing offline. The list shows when a link was last opened;
*Stop link* ends it at once. You are reminded the day before a link runs out.

## Shared markings

Bowings, breaths, cuts, dynamics – what everyone should have in their part. In the viewer's annotation tools, *Write
for* switches between *For me* and *For everyone in* the ensemble. Markings for everyone go to every member who plays
the part, beneath their own markings; they cannot change them and can hide them. Two ensembles playing the same work
keep separate markings. Players are told when there is something new (at most twice an hour).

## Recordings

On a work's page under **Recordings**, leaders of an ensemble with the work (and the library) add audio files: choose
*Kind* – *Practice recording of a part* (for a part, or for an instrument or voice) or *Full recording of the work* –
and *Origin* – *recorded* or *generated (e.g. from MIDI)* – then *Add audio files …*. Several files at once are fine.

Formats: MP3, M4A (also voice memos from a phone), WAV, FLAC, Ogg/Opus, WebM, AIFF – no videos. MIDI files cannot be
played directly yet: export them as audio from your notation software. Everyone who sees a part of the work hears
its recordings.
